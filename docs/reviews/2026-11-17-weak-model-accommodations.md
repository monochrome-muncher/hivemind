# What Hivemind carries for weak agent models, 2026-11-17

Baseline: main at 348fd71 (2.27.2 plus #462, #463). Premise from Monochrome: Hivemind's users run Claude-class agent models, not small local ones. Question: what exists mainly to accommodate weak models, and can it be trimmed or removed? The embedder (Qwen3-Embedding) is out of scope: it is the retrieval model and stays.

**The floor (Monochrome, 2026-11-17):** "strong" means the capacity of Qwen 3.8 27B or Gemma 4 26B and up, not only Claude. Everything built so far for weak models was built for Qwen3-14B, 8B or 4B, all below that floor. No model at the floor has been tested yet: Qwen3-14B already ran out of memory in this container, and the Hugging Face inference credits are used up (402), so the evidence below is Claude on one side and models under the floor on the other.

## Short answer

Less than it looks. Most of the always-on text is policy (what to write, what never to write, entries are data) or harness coverage (six harnesses never show the server `instructions`, so rules appear in more than one place on purpose). That stays whatever the model.

What really exists for weak models is two server-side write warnings, two clauses of tool text, and a habit of running Qwen live checks. The `tentative` warning is the clear win (`not_english` stays; see item 1): Claude models never drew them, the 14B model they were built for saw them and did not act, and one of them caused a quadratic-time bug.

## Does the floor change the calls?

Not for item 2 (item 1 is now kept for another reason; see its row). Their case rests on two facts that hold whatever the floor: the one weak model they were built for read the warning and did not act on it, so they never worked even below the floor; and a 26-27B model is closer to Haiku than to a 4-14B model at following a one-line rule in a tool description. It does change item 6: the before/after check should include one model at the floor when one can run. Options: top up the HF credits for a hosted Gemma 4 26B or Qwen 27B run, or run one on your machine through Remote Control. Until then, Haiku stands in as the floor.

## What it adds up to

- Code: about 40 lines of heuristics in `write_warnings.py` and about 6 tests go; one SPEC §4.1 row goes; one new ADR supersedes 0147 (and notes the switch from Qwen to Claude models for live checks).
- Always-on text: 17,429 B drops to about 17,265 B, roughly 165 B of new headroom under the 18,000 B budget. Modest: the budget pressure comes from policy, not from weak-model text.
- Contract: two warning codes stop appearing in write replies. Nothing in the agent text names them, so no skill change is needed; clients that ignore unknown codes are unaffected either way. CHANGELOG row: "two advisory write warnings removed; `hive_whoami` and `hive_search` descriptions shortened".
